# Lesson 2: Preflight Procedures and Safety

**Duration:** 90 minutes
**Prerequisites:** Lesson 1 - Introduction to Skydio X10 System

## Lesson Overview

This lesson covers the comprehensive preflight procedures required before every Skydio X10 flight. Proper preflight checks are essential for safe operations, equipment protection, and mission success. We'll cover regulations, safety protocols, and the complete preflight checklist.

## FAA Regulations Overview

### Part 107 Basics

**Key Regulations:**
- Must have Remote Pilot Certificate or fly under supervision of certified pilot
- Fly only in Class G airspace (or obtain authorization)
- Maximum altitude: 400 feet AGL (Above Ground Level)
- Maintain visual line of sight (VLOS)
- No operations over people (unless specific conditions met)
- Daylight operations only (without waiver)
- Maximum groundspeed: 100 mph

**Alaska Considerations:**
- Remote locations may have less congested airspace
- Local airports and seaplane bases require awareness
- Tribal lands may have additional restrictions
- Weather can change rapidly

**Required Actions:**
- Check NOTAM (Notice to Airmen) for temporary restrictions
- Verify airspace class using charts or apps (B4UFLY, AirMap)
- Obtain necessary authorizations
- Inform local authorities if required

### Registration

- Aircraft over 0.55 lbs must be registered with FAA
- Skydio X10 requires registration
- Registration number must be displayed
- Registration valid for 3 years

## Weather Assessment

### Critical Weather Factors

**Wind:**
- **Maximum safe wind:** 25-30 mph (Skydio X10 is wind-resistant)
- **Practical maximum:** 15-20 mph for thermal imaging and stable footage
- **Gusts:** More concerning than steady wind
- **Check:** Ground level AND altitude wind (often stronger aloft)

**Temperature:**
- **Operating range:** -20°C to +50°C (-4°F to +122°F)
- **Battery performance:** Reduced in cold weather
- **Optimal:** Above freezing for best battery life
- **Cold weather protocol:** Pre-warm batteries, reduce flight time

**Precipitation:**
- **Light rain:** X10 is weather-resistant but avoid when possible
- **Heavy rain:** Do not fly
- **Snow:** Light snow may be acceptable; heavy snow problematic
- **Moisture:** Can affect sensors and cameras

**Visibility:**
- **Minimum:** 3 statute miles
- **Must maintain:** Visual line of sight at all times
- **Fog/mist:** Can reduce visibility rapidly

**Cloud Ceiling:**
- Must maintain 500 feet below clouds
- Clouds can indicate weather changes

### Weather Resources

**Before Every Flight:**
1. **FAA Aviation Weather** - Aviation Weather Center (aviationweather.gov)
2. **Local AWOS/ASOS** - Automated weather stations (monitor on FAA radio)
3. **Weather Apps** - UAV Forecast, Hover, WindFinder
4. **Visual Assessment** - On-site conditions
5. **Forecast Trends** - Check if conditions improving or deteriorating

**Alaska-Specific Resources:**
- Alaska Aviation Weather Cameras (avcams.faa.gov)
- Local knowledge and traditional indicators
- Marine weather (coastal operations)

## Airspace Verification

### Before Flight

**Check Airspace:**
1. **Use B4UFLY App** - FAA's official app
2. **Check aeronautical charts** - Identify airspace classes
3. **Verify controlled airspace** - Class B, C, D requires authorization
4. **Check TFRs** - Temporary Flight Restrictions (wildfires, VIP movement)

**Quinhagak Airspace:**
- Identify local airport location and traffic pattern
- Note seaplane base operations
- Establish communication protocol if needed
- Maintain distance from runway approaches

**Communication:**
- Monitor CTAF (Common Traffic Advisory Frequency) on FAA radio
- Be aware of aircraft traffic
- Have plan to land immediately if aircraft approaches

### On-Site Pre-Flight Inspection

Reference: [Skydio X10 Flight Checklist](../resources/SkydioX10_Flight_Checklist_A0460.pdf)

#### Step 1: Visual Inspection of Aircraft

**Frame and Body:**
- [ ] Check for cracks, damage, or loose parts
- [ ] Inspect propeller guards
- [ ] Verify all covers and panels secure
- [ ] Check for debris or foreign objects

**Propellers:**
- [ ] Inspect each propeller for:
  - Cracks or chips
  - Proper attachment
  - No warping or bending
  - Clean condition
- [ ] Verify all propellers spin freely
- [ ] Check propeller guards intact

**Cameras and Sensors:**
- [ ] Clean visual camera lens
- [ ] Clean thermal camera lens
- [ ] Inspect navigation cameras (6 total)
- [ ] Verify no obstructions to sensors
- [ ] Check gimbal moves freely

**Battery Compartment:**
- [ ] Inspect battery contacts (clean, no corrosion)
- [ ] Check compartment for debris
- [ ] Verify battery release mechanism works

#### Step 2: Battery Inspection

**Battery Check:**
- [ ] Verify battery fully charged (LED indicators)
- [ ] Check for physical damage
- [ ] Look for swelling or deformation
- [ ] Inspect contacts
- [ ] Feel for excessive warmth (before installation)
- [ ] Verify firmware version compatible

**Battery Installation:**
- [ ] Insert battery firmly until click
- [ ] Verify secure seating
- [ ] Check LED status lights
- [ ] Confirm battery locked in place

#### Step 3: Controller Inspection

**Flight Deck Controller:**
- [ ] Power on controller
- [ ] Verify battery charge level (>50%)
- [ ] Check screen for damage/clarity
- [ ] Test all buttons and sticks
- [ ] Verify stick calibration
- [ ] Check antenna position

**Connection:**
- [ ] Establish link with aircraft
- [ ] Verify telemetry data displaying
- [ ] Check GPS signal strength
- [ ] Confirm compass calibration
- [ ] Review no error messages

#### Step 4: Camera System Check

**Visual Camera:**
- [ ] Power on drone
- [ ] Check camera feed on controller
- [ ] Verify image clarity
- [ ] Test gimbal movement (pitch, yaw)
- [ ] Check camera settings correct

**Thermal Camera:**
- [ ] Switch to thermal view
- [ ] Verify thermal image displaying
- [ ] Check temperature scale settings
- [ ] Test color palette options
- [ ] Verify radiometric data if needed

#### Step 5: System Status

**Aircraft Systems:**
- [ ] GPS status: Adequate satellites (6+ required, 10+ ideal)
- [ ] IMU status: Calibrated
- [ ] Compass: Calibrated (if prompted)
- [ ] Firmware: Up to date
- [ ] Obstacle avoidance: Active and functioning
- [ ] Home point: Set correctly

**Warnings and Errors:**
- [ ] Review any system warnings
- [ ] Clear or address all errors
- [ ] Verify "Ready to Fly" status
- [ ] Check no critical alerts

#### Step 6: Flight Area Assessment

**Site Survey:**
- [ ] Identify obstacles (power lines, trees, buildings)
- [ ] Note wind direction and speed
- [ ] Verify adequate takeoff/landing area
- [ ] Check for people or animals in area
- [ ] Identify emergency landing zones
- [ ] Assess lighting conditions

**Safety Perimeter:**
- [ ] Establish safety perimeter
- [ ] Brief any observers/assistants
- [ ] Position spotter if needed
- [ ] Verify clear of people/vehicles
- [ ] Post warning signs if appropriate

#### Step 7: Final Checks

**Before Takeoff:**
- [ ] Pilot ready (rested, focused)
- [ ] Spotter ready and briefed
- [ ] Communication protocol established
- [ ] Emergency procedures reviewed
- [ ] Return-to-home point verified
- [ ] Flight plan reviewed
- [ ] Controller settings confirmed
- [ ] Camera settings configured

## Go / No-Go Decision

### Flight Should NOT Proceed If:

**Weather:**
- ❌ Wind exceeds safe limits
- ❌ Precipitation present
- ❌ Poor visibility
- ❌ Rapidly deteriorating conditions

**Equipment:**
- ❌ Any damage to aircraft
- ❌ Low battery levels
- ❌ System errors or warnings
- ❌ GPS signal insufficient
- ❌ Camera malfunction

**Airspace:**
- ❌ No authorization for controlled airspace
- ❌ TFR (Temporary Flight Restriction) in effect
- ❌ Aircraft traffic unsafe

**Site:**
- ❌ People cannot be cleared from area
- ❌ Excessive hazards present
- ❌ Inadequate emergency landing options

**Pilot:**
- ❌ Pilot fatigued or distracted
- ❌ Pilot uncomfortable with conditions
- ❌ Inadequate planning

**When in doubt, don't fly. It's always better to postpone than to take unnecessary risks.**

## Emergency Procedures

### Return to Home (RTH)

**When to Use:**
- Lost visual contact
- Low battery warning
- Controller signal loss
- Changing weather conditions
- Any emergency situation

**How to Activate:**
- **Automatic:** Triggered by low battery or signal loss
- **Manual:** RTH button on controller
- **Verify home point** set correctly before takeoff

**RTH Behavior:**
- Aircraft climbs to preset altitude
- Returns to home point via direct path
- Descends and attempts landing
- Obstacle avoidance active during RTH

### Emergency Landing

**Forced Landing:**
- If battery critical, land immediately
- Choose safest available area
- Avoid people and structures
- Maintain control until touchdown
- Reduce altitude quickly but controlled

**Lost Signal:**
- Aircraft will initiate RTH automatically
- Stay in location where signal lost
- Wait for aircraft to return
- Do not move vehicle if launched from vehicle
- Have spotter monitor approach

### Fly-Away Prevention

**If Aircraft Not Responding:**
- Don't panic
- Try RTH function
- Check controller connection
- Verify GPS mode active
- If all else fails, power down (last resort)

## Cold Weather Operations (Alaska-Specific)

### Battery Management in Cold

**Before Flight:**
- Pre-warm batteries (keep in warm pocket, vehicle, or warming bag)
- Don't install cold battery into aircraft
- Target battery temperature: 15-20°C (60-70°F) before use

**During Flight:**
- Battery will warm during operation
- Monitor voltage closely
- Expect 20-30% reduction in flight time
- Land with higher reserve (25-30% instead of 20%)

**After Flight:**
- Warm battery slowly (don't rapid heat)
- Store at room temperature
- Check for damage from cold

### Cold Weather Effects

**Aircraft:**
- Reduced battery performance
- Slower response time initially
- Propellers may be stiff at startup
- Lubrication affected by extreme cold

**Pilot:**
- Warm gloves (but maintain dexterity)
- Protect controller from extreme cold
- Screen may be less responsive in cold
- Reduced manual dexterity

**Environment:**
- Ice on sensors possible
- Snow can obscure landing area
- Wind chill affects comfort
- Daylight hours limited in winter

- **Never skip preflight checks** - They exist for good reasons
