# Module 7: Remote Sensing and Drone Operations

## Introduction to Skydio X10 for Community Mapping and Monitoring

**Duration:** 2-3 days

## Overview

This module introduces you to Unmanned Aerial Vehicle (UAV/drone) operations using the Skydio X10 platform for community mapping, infrastructure inspection, and thermal monitoring. You'll learn the fundamentals of the Skydio X10 system, flight operations, thermal imaging applications, and how to integrate drone-collected data into your GIS workflows.

By the end of this module, you'll understand how to safely operate the Skydio X10, conduct thermal inspections, collect high-resolution imagery, and use aerial data for community monitoring and decision-making.

## 💡 Tips for Success

### Flight Operations
- **Always complete preflight checklist** - Never skip steps, even if you're experienced
- **Hand launch/catch requires practice** - Start in calm conditions
- **Monitor battery levels** - Land with at least 20% remaining
- **Check wind conditions** - Skydio can handle wind, but know your limits
- **Use obstacle avoidance** - Trust the system but maintain awareness
- **Keep visual line of sight** - Always know where your drone is
- **Practice Manual Skills mode** - Builds confidence and skill

### Thermal Imaging
- **Timing matters** - Best results at dawn/dusk or night with temperature differential
- **Inside/outside temperature difference** - Ideal is 20°F (11°C) or more
- **Weather conditions** - Calm, clear conditions are best
- **Distance and angle** - Maintain consistent distance for comparison
- **Multiple perspectives** - Capture all sides of buildings
- **Document settings** - Record camera settings for consistency
- **Compare similar buildings** - Helps identify outliers

### Controller Mastery
- **Customize settings** - Configure for your flying style
- **Learn the menus** - Know where everything is before flight
- **Watch telemetry** - Monitor altitude, distance, battery constantly
- **Practice mode switches** - Be comfortable switching between modes
- **Haptic feedback** - Pay attention to controller vibrations/alerts

### Safety First
- **Weather is critical** - Don't fly in conditions beyond your skill level
- **Listen to FAA radio** - Awareness of aircraft traffic is essential
- **Have a spotter** - Extra eyes enhance safety
- **Know your limits** - It's okay to postpone or cancel flights
- **Emergency procedures** - Practice return-to-home and emergency landing

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

### Common Issues

**Skydio X10 won't power on**
- Check battery is fully seated
- Verify battery charge level
- Inspect battery contacts for debris
- Try different battery
- [See Operator Manual pages 15-17](./resources/samples/SkydioX10_Operator_Manual_A0582_Battin-compressed.pdf)

**Poor GPS signal**
- Allow time for GPS acquisition (1-2 minutes)
- Move to open area away from buildings
- Check for GPS interference
- Verify GPS settings in controller
- [See detailed guide →](./troubleshooting/gps-issues.md)

**Obstacle avoidance too sensitive**
- Adjust sensitivity settings in Flight Deck
- Check for dirt/debris on sensors
- Verify lighting conditions (avoidance affected by low light)
- Review obstacle avoidance configuration
- [See detailed guide →](./troubleshooting/obstacle-avoidance.md)

**Thermal image quality issues**
- Check thermal camera is clean
- Verify temperature differential exists
- Adjust thermal palette settings
- Check distance to subject
- Review environmental conditions
- [See detailed guide →](./troubleshooting/thermal-imaging.md)

**Hand catch problems**
- Approach from below
- Use firm, confident grip
- Keep fingers away from propellers
- Practice with spotter assistance
- Review hand catch procedures
- [See detailed guide →](./troubleshooting/hand-catch.md)

**Controller connection issues**
- Verify controller is powered on
- Check signal strength
- Reduce distance to aircraft
- Restart controller and aircraft
- [See detailed guide →](./troubleshooting/controller-connection.md)

## Case Study: Quinhagak Building Heat Loss Assessment

### Background
Many homes and buildings in Quinhagak experience high heating costs due to heat loss. Identifying where heat is escaping helps prioritize weatherization efforts and reduce energy costs for residents and community facilities.

### Approach
Use Skydio X10 thermal imaging to:
- Inspect residential homes
- Assess commercial and public buildings
- Identify major heat loss areas
- Prioritize weatherization investments
- Support funding applications for energy programs

### Methodology
- **Equipment:** Skydio X10 with thermal camera
- **Timing:** Evening flights (6-9 PM) with indoor heating active
- **Temperature differential:** -10°F outside, +65°F inside (75°F difference)
- **Flight parameters:** 30-50 feet from buildings, multiple angles
- **Documentation:** Thermal and visual imagery of all building faces

### Findings
**Residential Buildings:**
- Significant heat loss through windows (single-pane)
- Door frames showing thermal leaks
- Roof insulation gaps visible
- Foundation perimeter heat loss
- Attic ventilation issues

**Commercial/Public Buildings:**
- Larger heat loss areas due to building size
- HVAC system inefficiencies visible
- Warehouse doors major loss points
- Skylight heat escape
- Ventilation system heat signatures

### Outcomes
- **30+ buildings assessed** in single evening
- **Heat loss severity ranking** created
- **Prioritization list** for weatherization program
- **Visual evidence** for grant applications
- **Cost savings projections** based on thermal data
- **Community awareness** increased about energy efficiency

### Impact
- Tribal council approved weatherization program
- Federal grant application submitted with thermal imagery
- Homeowners requesting energy audits
- School building improvements prioritized
- Estimated 15-30% potential heating cost reduction

### Lessons Learned
- Evening thermal flights highly effective in Alaska winter
- Temperature differential critical for clear imagery
- Multiple angles provide comprehensive assessment
- Visual imagery needed alongside thermal for context
- Community members interested in seeing their own homes
- Data supports both immediate action and long-term planning
