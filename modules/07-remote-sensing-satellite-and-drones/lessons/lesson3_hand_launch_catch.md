# Lesson 3: Hand Launch and Hand Catch Techniques

**Duration:** 60 minutes
**Prerequisites:** Lesson 2 - Preflight Procedures and Safety

---

## Lesson Overview

Hand launching and hand catching are essential skills for operating the Skydio X10 in environments without suitable landing areas. This lesson covers safe techniques for launching the drone from your hand and catching it for landing, which is particularly useful in Alaska's varied terrain.

---

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Understand when and why to use hand launch/catch techniques
2. Identify safety considerations for hand operations
3. Execute a safe hand launch procedure
4. Perform a controlled hand catch landing
5. Avoid common mistakes and hazards
6. Adapt techniques for different weather conditions
7. Practice emergency hand catch scenarios

---

## When to Use Hand Launch and Hand Catch

### Situations Requiring Hand Launch/Catch

**Terrain Challenges:**
- Rocky or uneven ground
- Snow-covered areas
- Wet or muddy surfaces
- Vegetation (tall grass, tundra)
- Ice
- Sand or gravel that could damage propellers

**Environmental Conditions:**
- Limited flat landing areas
- Remote field locations
- Beach operations (avoid sand ingestion)
- Marsh or wetland areas
- Mountainous terrain

**Operational Needs:**
- Quick deployment required
- Boat or vessel operations
- Moving vehicle launches (with proper authorization)
- Urban environments with limited space
- Protecting camera/sensors from ground debris

**Quinhagak Applications:**
- Coastal surveys on beaches
- Tundra operations
- Winter operations on snow/ice
- Remote site inspections
- Boat-based surveys

## Safety Considerations

### Critical Safety Rules

**Before Attempting Hand Launch/Catch:**

1. **Complete preflight checklist** - Never skip
2. **Practice with instructor supervision first**
3. **Start in calm weather conditions**
4. **Wear appropriate clothing:**
   - No loose clothing that could catch
   - Close-fitting gloves if needed
   - Eye protection recommended
   - Remove dangling jewelry

5. **Clear area** - No bystanders within 20 feet
6. **Firm footing** - Stable stance essential
7. **Full concentration** - No distractions

### Hazards to Avoid

**Propeller Contact:**
- ⚠️ **Active propellers can cause injury**
- Keep fingers away from prop guards during spin-up
- Never reach over or around spinning propellers
- Wait for full propeller stop before handling aircraft after landing

**Loss of Control:**
- Strong grip on aircraft during launch
- Stable platform for catch
- Don't attempt in high winds until experienced
- Clear understanding of abort procedures

**Environmental:**
- Wind gusts can affect stability
- Slippery surfaces reduce control
- Cold reduces manual dexterity
- Bright sun can make it hard to see aircraft

## Hand Launch Procedure

### Setup and Preparation

**Pre-Launch Checks:**
1. Complete full preflight checklist
2. Power on controller (Flight Deck)
3. Power on aircraft
4. Verify GPS lock (wait for adequate satellites)
5. Check home point set correctly
6. Verify "Ready to Fly" status
7. Configure flight mode (Manual Skills recommended for launch)
8. Ensure camera gimbal initialized

**Positioning:**
- Stand with feet shoulder-width apart
- Face into the wind (if wind present)
- Clear area all around (360°)
- Spotter positioned safely behind/beside you
- Verify no overhead obstacles

### Launching Sequence

**Step 1: Grip the Aircraft**
- Hold drone firmly with palm under the body
- Grip on the sides, avoiding propellers
- Thumb and fingers on frame, not prop guards
- Aircraft should feel secure in hand
- Don't squeeze too hard (avoid triggering sensors)

**Step 2: Raise to Launch Position**
- Extend arm fully
- Hold aircraft away from body (arm's length)
- Level the aircraft (don't tilt)
- Height: Chest to head level
- Ensure clear space above for propeller spool

**Step 3: Arm the Motors**
- Use controller to arm motors (follow Skydio procedure)
- **Listen for propeller spool-up**
- **Feel for vibration increasing**
- Maintain firm grip
- Keep aircraft level

**Step 4: Release**
- When propellers at full speed:
  - Give gentle upward push (6-12 inches)
  - Release cleanly (don't throw)
  - Step back immediately
  - Hands down and clear

**Step 5: Take Control**
- Aircraft should hover momentarily
- Take positive control with sticks
- Climb to safe altitude (10-15 feet minimum)
- Verify stable flight
- Proceed with mission

### Common Hand Launch Mistakes

**❌ Mistake:** Releasing too early (before full spool-up)
**✅ Fix:** Wait for full throttle, feel the lift

**❌ Mistake:** Throwing the aircraft upward
**✅ Fix:** Gentle push or simply release, let props do the work

**❌ Mistake:** Tilting aircraft during release
**✅ Fix:** Keep level, release evenly

**❌ Mistake:** Not stepping back quickly
**✅ Fix:** Immediately create distance after release

**❌ Mistake:** Weak or hesitant grip
**✅ Fix:** Confident, firm hold until release moment

## Hand Catch Procedure

### Approach and Preparation

**Pre-Catch Setup:**
1. Identify safe catching location
2. Clear area of obstacles and people
3. Position yourself with stable footing
4. Remove or secure any loose items
5. Brief spotter on procedure
6. Plan abort/emergency landing area

**Positioning:**
- Feet shoulder-width apart
- Stable, balanced stance
- Clear overhead space
- Downwind of aircraft if wind present
- Spotter ready to assist if needed

### Catching Sequence

**Step 1: Bring Aircraft to Position**
- Use controller to position drone
- Height: 3-5 feet above your head initially
- Distance: 6-10 feet in front
- Verify aircraft stable and responding
- Check wind effect on hover

**Step 2: Lower Aircraft**
- Slowly descend to head height
- Keep aircraft centered in front of you
- Maintain steady hover
- Monitor for wind gusts
- Have aircraft level (not tilted)

**Step 3: Final Approach**
- Lower to chest height
- Distance: Arm's length away
- Aircraft should be rock-steady
- Take a breath, focus
- Prepare your catching hand

**Step 4: The Catch**
- **Extend arm toward aircraft**
- **Approach from below the aircraft**
- **Reach up under the drone body**
- **Grasp firmly on sides/center body**
- **Avoid reaching over or near propellers**

**Critical: Never reach from above or sides into propellers**

**Step 5: Disarm**
- With aircraft firmly in hand
- Use controller to disarm motors (follow Skydio procedure)
- **Keep grip until propellers fully stopped**
- Wait for complete stop (2-3 seconds)
- Lower aircraft safely

**Step 6: Secure**
- Hold aircraft securely
- Check propellers completely stopped
- Power down aircraft
- Power down controller
- Place in case or safe location

### Common Hand Catch Mistakes

**❌ Mistake:** Reaching from above into spinning propellers
**✅ Fix:** ALWAYS approach from below, hand up under body

**❌ Mistake:** Grabbing too early while aircraft still descending
**✅ Fix:** Let aircraft stabilize at catch height, then reach

**❌ Mistake:** Weak grip causing drop
**✅ Fix:** Confident, firm grasp on body

**❌ Mistake:** Disarming before secure grip
**✅ Fix:** Catch first, then disarm

**❌ Mistake:** Flinching or pulling away
**✅ Fix:** Commit to the catch, steady hand

**❌ Mistake:** Catching at wrong height (too high or too low)
**✅ Fix:** Chest height ideal, comfortable reach

## Advanced Techniques

### Catching in Wind

**Challenges:**
- Aircraft may drift during approach
- Harder to maintain steady hover
- Requires precise control

**Technique:**
- Lower aircraft more slowly
- Account for drift
- Position yourself to intercept
- Be ready to adjust position
- Higher skill level required

**When NOT to Catch in Wind:**
- Winds over 15 mph (for beginners)
- Gusty conditions
- If not confident
- Emergency landing area available

### One-Handed Operations

**When Used:**
- Operating controller with one hand
- Quick catches
- Experienced operators only

**Technique:**
- Position aircraft precisely
- Controller in one hand (maintain control)
- Catching hand ready
- Catch, then immediately disarm
- Requires significant practice

**Not Recommended for Beginners**

### Emergency Hand Catch

**Situations Requiring Emergency Catch:**
- Battery critical and no landing area
- Equipment malfunction requiring immediate landing
- Unexpected weather change
- People/animals entering planned landing zone

**Procedure:**
- **Stay calm**
- Position aircraft as best as possible
- Accept less-than-perfect conditions
- Prioritize safety over smooth technique
- Catch may be rougher than planned
- Focus on stopping propellers quickly

## Practice Progression

### Training Sequence

**Level 1: Ground Familiarization** (5 minutes)
- Handle aircraft with propellers off
- Practice grip positions
- Practice hand positions for catch
- Understand where to grab safely

**Level 2: Assisted Launch with Instructor** (10 minutes)
- Instructor supervises closely
- Launch in calm conditions
- Immediate climb after release
- Land conventionally (not hand catch)

**Level 3: Assisted Catch with Instructor** (10 minutes)
- Instructor positions aircraft
- Student practices catch
- Multiple repetitions
- Instructor can take over if needed

**Level 4: Full Cycle with Supervision** (15 minutes)
- Student performs launch
- Brief hover
- Student performs catch
- Instructor observes and coaches

**Level 5: Independent Operations** (Ongoing)
- Demonstrate proficiency
- Instructor sign-off
- Continue to practice
- Build confidence

### Practice Goals

**Minimum Proficiency:**
- 5 successful hand launches
- 5 successful hand catches
- Demonstrated in calm conditions
- No safety violations
- Comfortable and confident

**Ongoing Practice:**
- Regular practice maintains skills
- Gradually increase difficulty (light wind)
- Different environments
- Emergency scenarios

## Cold Weather Hand Operations

### Alaska-Specific Considerations

**Challenges:**
- Reduced manual dexterity
- Cold affects reaction time
- Snow/ice creates slippery footing
- Gloves reduce grip and feel
- Wind chill affects pilot

**Solutions:**
- **Gloves:** Thin, grippy gloves vs. heavy mittens
- **Warm-up:** Keep hands warm before launch/catch
- **Footing:** Clear snow/ice from launch area
- **Practice:** Extra practice with gloves on
- **Buddy system:** Spotter assistance

**Equipment Considerations:**
- Battery pre-warming (launch soon after installation)
- Propellers may be stiff in extreme cold
- Allow extra spool-up time
- Monitor aircraft response

---

## Emergency Procedures

### Abort Hand Launch

**If Something Goes Wrong During Launch:**
- **Before release:** Power down immediately
- **Just after release:** Let aircraft auto-stabilize and climb, then land conventionally
- **Never try to re-grab during launch**

### Abort Hand Catch

**If Catch Going Wrong:**
- **Increase altitude immediately**
- **Abort catch attempt**
- **Re-position aircraft**
- **Try again or land conventionally**
- **Never grab at unstable aircraft**

### Lost Control During Hand Operations

**If Aircraft Not Responding:**
- **Don't panic**
- **Step away**
- **Let obstacle avoidance work**
- **Try Return-to-Home**
- **Clear the area**

## Key Takeaways

- **Hand launch/catch are essential skills** for operations in challenging terrain
- **Safety is paramount** - propellers can cause injury
- **Always approach from below** when hand catching
- **Practice builds confidence** - start with supervision
- **Firm grip and stable stance** are critical for both launch and catch
- **Know when to abort** - don't force a bad catch
- **Cold weather requires adaptation** - gloves, footing, warm hands
- **Never skip preflight checks** even for quick hand launch operations
- **Spotter assistance recommended** especially when learning
