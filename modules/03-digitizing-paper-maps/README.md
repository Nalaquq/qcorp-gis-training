# Module 3: Digitizing Paper Maps

## Preserving Historical Maps and Records for Community Use

**Duration:** 1 day
**Prerequisites:** Module 1 (ArcGIS Online Basics)
**Equipment Required:** DSLR camera, tripod, color card, computer with ArcGIS Pro
**Setting:** Qanirtuuq Land Manager's Office
**Training Date:** October 2025

---

## Overview

This module teaches you how to digitize paper maps from the land manager's office and make them accessible in GIS. You'll learn professional photography techniques for map documentation, understand file formats, and master the critical skill of georeferencing historical maps to make them usable in modern GIS.

By the end of this module, you'll have contributed to preserving Quinhagak's historical land records and making them accessible to the community through ArcGIS Online.

---

## Real-World Scenario

**The Challenge:** The Qanirtuuq Land Manager's office contains decades of paper maps - ANCSA surveys, land use plans, infrastructure maps, and historical records. These valuable documents are:
- Deteriorating over time
- Difficult to access and share
- Not integrated with modern GIS
- At risk of being lost

**The Solution:** Digitize these maps, georeference them to real-world coordinates, and share them through ArcGIS Online so the community can access this critical historical information.

**The Reality:** In one day of training, students successfully organized the land manager's office and digitized approximately 40 maps, making them available through an ArcGIS Online Group.

**View the Results:** https://arcg.is/0H8S1y1

## 📚 Resources

### Camera and Photography

**Equipment:**
- Sony Alpha DSLR (or similar)
- Tripod (essential for document photography)
- Color card (for white balance)
- Copy stand or overhead setup (ideal but not required)
- Even lighting (natural light or soft artificial light)

**Camera Settings for Map Photography:**
- Mode: Manual or Aperture Priority
- ISO: Lowest (100-200 for best quality)
- Aperture: f/8 to f/11 (good depth of field, sharp image)
- Shutter Speed: Adjust for proper exposure (tripod allows slow shutter)
- White Balance: Custom (using color card)
- Format: RAW (.dng) for maximum quality, JPEG for convenience

**Photography Best Practices:**
- Use tripod to eliminate camera shake
- Position camera parallel to map (avoid keystoning)
- Use even lighting without glare
- Include color card in first shot for white balance
- Fill frame with map (but capture all edges)
- Check focus before shooting
- Photograph map flat (use weights if needed)
- Take multiple shots if uncertain

### File Formats

**RAW (.dng - Digital Negative):**
- **Pros:** Maximum quality, full editing flexibility, preserves all data
- **Cons:** Large file size, requires processing, not all software supports
- **Use for:** Archival masters, maps needing color correction

**TIFF (.tiff):**
- **Pros:** High quality, lossless, widely supported, good for GIS
- **Cons:** Large file size
- **Use for:** Processed maps ready for georeferencing, archival storage

**JPEG (.jpg):**
- **Pros:** Small file size, universal support, easy to share
- **Cons:** Lossy compression, quality degrades with editing
- **Use for:** Web sharing, quick reference, ArcGIS Online uploads

**Recommended Workflow:**
1. Shoot in RAW (.dng)
2. Process and export as TIFF (archival)
3. Create JPEG for web sharing
4. Upload TIFF or JPEG to ArcGIS Pro for georeferencing

### Georeferencing Resources

**Official Documentation:**
- [Georeference a Raster in ArcGIS Pro](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/data/imagery/overview-of-georeferencing.htm)
- [Fundamentals of Georeferencing](https://pro.arcgis.com/en/pro-app/latest/help/data/imagery/fundamentals-for-georeferencing-a-raster-dataset.htm)

**Control Points:**
- **Good control points:**
  - Permanent structures (buildings that haven't moved)
  - Road intersections
  - Bridge locations
  - Shoreline features (if stable)
  - Survey monuments
  - PLSS section corners

- **Poor control points:**
  - Temporary structures
  - Vegetation (changes over time)
  - Eroding shorelines
  - Features that may have moved

**Minimum Requirements:**
- At least 3 control points (4+ recommended)
- Points distributed across map (not clustered)
- Points with high certainty
- RMS error reviewed and acceptable

**Transformation Types:**
- **1st Order Polynomial (Affine):** Rotation, scale, skew - use for most maps
- **2nd Order Polynomial:** Adds curvature - use for large areas
- **3rd Order Polynomial:** Complex distortion - use sparingly
- **Spline:** Exact fit to control points - use for severely warped maps

### ANCSA and PLSS Background

**ANCSA 14(c):**
- Alaska Native Claims Settlement Act
- Section 14(c) conveys lands to villages
- Historical survey maps show conveyed lands
- Critical for land ownership documentation

**PLSS (Public Land Survey System):**
- Township and Range grid system
- Used across much of Alaska
- Section = 1 mile × 1 mile (usually)
- Townships = 6 miles × 6 miles
- Sections numbered 1-36
- Survey monuments mark corners

**Resources:**
- [Alaska PLSS Data](https://www.blm.gov/programs/national-cadastral-survey/cadastral-survey/cadastral-survey-records)
- [ANCSA Information](https://www.blm.gov/programs/lands-and-realty/regional-information/alaska/alaska-native-claims-settlement-act)

## 💡 Tips for Success

### Photography Tips

- **Lighting is everything**
  - Use indirect natural light when possible
  - Avoid glare from glossy maps
  - Shadows indicate uneven lighting
  - Diffuse harsh light sources

- **Camera stability**
  - Always use tripod
  - Use timer or remote to avoid camera shake
  - Check focus carefully
  - Take multiple shots for safety

- **Positioning**
  - Keep camera parallel to map
  - Fill frame but capture all content
  - Include scale bar if present
  - Photograph legend/title block

- **Color accuracy**
  - Include color card in first shot
  - Use custom white balance
  - Consistent lighting across session
  - Process RAW files for best color

### Georeferencing Tips

- **Know your map**
  - What year was it made?
  - What features were present then?
  - What might have changed?
  - What coordinate system might it use?

- **Use local knowledge**
  - You know Quinhagak better than anyone!
  - Identify features from your own experience
  - Ask elders about historical locations
  - Verify uncertain features

- **Start simple**
  - Add obvious control points first
  - Add more points if accuracy poor
  - Distributed points better than clustered
  - 6-10 points usually sufficient

- **Check your work**
  - Does the map align with basemap?
  - Do known features match up?
  - Is RMS error acceptable (< 5 pixels)?
  - Test against known locations

### Organization Tips

- **Create system from start**
  - Consistent naming conventions
  - Organized folder structure
  - Track what's been digitized
  - Document process

- **Prioritize wisely**
  - Community need comes first
  - Deteriorating maps are urgent
  - Unique information is valuable
  - Easy wins build momentum

- **Think long-term**
  - Sustainable organization system
  - Train multiple people
  - Document procedures
  - Plan for ongoing digitization

## 🔧 Troubleshooting

### Photography Issues

**Problem:** Images are blurry
- **Cause:** Camera shake, poor focus
- **Solution:** Use tripod, check focus, increase shutter speed

**Problem:** Glare on map
- **Cause:** Direct lighting, glossy map surface
- **Solution:** Diffuse light, angle lights, use polarizing filter

**Problem:** Colors look wrong
- **Cause:** Incorrect white balance
- **Solution:** Use color card, set custom white balance

**Problem:** Map edges dark
- **Cause:** Uneven lighting, vignetting
- **Solution:** Adjust light position, use smaller aperture

### Georeferencing Issues

**Problem:** Control points won't place accurately
- **Cause:** Wrong zoom level, basemap doesn't match
- **Solution:** Zoom in closer, try different basemap, verify feature exists

**Problem:** High RMS error
- **Cause:** Poor control points, incorrect feature identification
- **Solution:** Review control points, remove outliers, add better points

**Problem:** Map stretches or warps incorrectly
- **Cause:** Wrong transformation type, bad control points
- **Solution:** Use 1st order polynomial, redistribute control points

**Problem:** Can't find control points
- **Cause:** Map too old, area changed significantly
- **Solution:** Use PLSS grid, find least-changed features, research historical features

### Upload Issues

**Problem:** File too large to upload
- **Cause:** High resolution TIFF
- **Solution:** Compress as JPEG, reduce resolution, tile large maps

**Problem:** Georeferencing lost after upload
- **Cause:** No .aux files or world file
- **Solution:** Export as georeferenced TIFF from ArcGIS Pro

**Problem:** Can't share to Group
- **Cause:** Permissions settings
- **Solution:** Check Group settings, verify membership, adjust sharing
